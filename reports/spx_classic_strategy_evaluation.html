Research-priority conclusion. Turtle remains the strongest daily candidate because it combines positive full-history and holdout estimates with the highest rule authenticity, but its holdout contains only ten non-overlapping events and a wide interval. Dual Thrust is the strongest intraday candidate, but only six holdout events are available. Escalator has the largest holdout point estimate, yet its rule attribution is weak and its interval also crosses zero. These are promotion candidates for fuller native-strategy backtests, not validated edges.

What was actually tested

Daily outcome definition

  • 2,512 SPX daily OHLC bars, 26 Aug 2016–25 Aug 2026.
  • Signal observed at close; entry at next observation.
  • Fixed horizon: 10 bars.
  • Triple barrier: +3% target, −2% stop, 20-bar maximum; the 2% ex-ante stop is one R.
  • Development ends 21 Aug 2024; the holdout begins 22 Aug 2024. Crossing outcomes are purged.
  • High/low touches determine barriers; a same-bar target/stop ambiguity is conservatively a loss.

Intraday outcome definition

  • 781 SPX 30-minute OHLC bars, 1 Jun–25 Aug 2026.
  • Signal observed at bar close; entry one bar later.
  • Fixed horizon: 2 bars (one hour).
  • Triple barrier: +0.50% target, −0.35% stop, 4-bar maximum; the 0.35% ex-ante stop is one R.
  • Development ends 7 Aug 2026; the holdout begins 10 Aug 2026. Crossing outcomes are purged.
  • Only signals with the complete forward horizon inside the same New York session were evaluated.
Not a native-strategy backtest. The common evaluator deliberately ignores Turtle unit sizing/pyramiding, gap-fill exits, end-of-day liquidation, ATR-native stops and Martingale doubling. This isolates entry signal quality, but it cannot answer which full trading system would produce the best portfolio return. SPX is an index, not a directly executable instrument; commissions, slippage, futures basis, ETF tracking, borrow and taxes are absent.

Each summary uses 1,000 deterministic circular moving-block resamples of the non-overlapping event sequence and 200 matched random-timestamp baselines preserving completed event count, direction mix, risk declarations and causal timestamp eligibility. Intraday controls obey the same complete-forward-horizon, same-session rule as actual signals. “Bootstrap > 0” is the fraction of resampled means above zero, not a posterior probability. Yahoo data were fetched through the bounded provider adapter and raw payloads were not committed. Daily and intraday cohorts must not be compared as though they share the same sample length, volatility scale or effective number of independent observations.

Rule audit: all ten systems

Turtle Trading

High rule fidelity
01

Implemented: close above/below the prior 20-day high/low while flat; position state resets through the opposite 10-day channel. Signals exclude the current bar from the channel.

Source audit: the core 20/10 rules match published Turtle System 1, although the full system also includes the prior-winning-breakout filter, 55-day failsafe, 2N stop, ½N pyramiding and volatility-normalized units. See the published rule summary and an open-source implementation.

SPX read: +0.210R full-history and +0.065R holdout EV; both intervals include zero. Decision: advance to a dedicated native-system prototype, not a validated strategy.

Gap Trading

Generic rule
02

Implemented: contrarian long when today’s open is below the prior low by 0.5× prior ATR14; symmetric short above the prior high.

Source audit: “gap trading” is a family rather than one canonical branded system. The thread says opening gap but writes Low/High; the implementation uses the open to avoid lookahead. CME documents that index openings can exhibit material gaps, but that does not establish a universal fill edge (SOQ discussion).

SPX read: −0.005R full-history and +0.027R holdout EV; evidence remains inconclusive. Decision: keep as a configurable research template, not a promoted edge.

Dolphin Trading

Article proxy
03

Implemented: 30-minute EMA5 > EMA20 plus close above the prior two bars’ highs; symmetric short version added for comparison.

Source audit: public “Dolphin” descriptions use multi-timeframe alignment, KD/MACD and materially different moving averages—not this EMA5/20 rule. A Chinese description, for example, lists MA26/52/104/360 and multi-timeframe KD/MACD (source). The brand attribution is therefore weak.

SPX read: +0.068R full-history but negative independent holdout EV. Decision: retain as “EMA momentum breakout,” not as authenticated Dolphin.

Martingale

Not scored
04

Why no signal: doubling after a loss changes exposure conditional on prior outcomes; it does not specify an independent direction. Attaching it to an arbitrary base signal would answer a different question.

Risk audit: finite capital and position limits make a sufficiently long losing streak terminal. Recent decision-science research states the failure mechanism plainly: eventually the bettor can no longer double and may lose everything (Cambridge Core).

Decision: exclude from the signal skill; if studied later, use a separate path-dependent capital/ruin simulator with hard risk limits.

R-Breaker

Simplified proxy
05

Implemented: prior-session pivot ±0.35×range breakouts, plus a reversal after an excursion through pivot ±one range and recovery through the nearer line.

Source audit: commonly published R-Breaker rules derive six setup, entry and breakout levels with several parameters, not only the thread’s two-line shorthand. Compare the six-level formulas and pivot-grid implementation.

SPX read: −0.071R full-history EV, PF 0.81 on 43 events. Decision: re-specify canonical six-level behavior before any skill promotion.

Dual Thrust

Article variant
06

Implemented: current session open ±0.5×maximum of the prior two daily high-low ranges; first qualifying intraday close generates the direction.

Source audit: the more widely published Michael Chalek formula is Range = max(HH−LC, HC−LL) over N days, so the thread’s two-range calculation is a variant (formula discussion; open-source example).

SPX read: strongest intraday point estimate, +0.165R full-history and +0.047R holdout EV, but only six holdout events. Decision: prototype after selecting and versioning one formula.

Fairy Four Price

Framework corroborated
07

Implemented: first intraday close above the previous session high or below the previous session low.

Source audit: public Chinese strategy material corroborates yesterday high/low/close plus today open and prior-extreme breakout, while also warning the named framework was summarized by later users rather than stated directly by the trader (FMZ; WonderTrader documentation).

SPX read: modest +0.041R full-history but −0.053R holdout EV. Decision: watchlist; requires longer intraday history and exact exit treatment.

“Oliver-Kell” EMA

Misattributed simplification
08

Implemented: mechanical EMA5/20 crossover, long on the upward cross and short on the downward cross.

Source audit: Oliver Kell’s own public framework describes a cycle of price action—Wedge Pop, EMA Crossback, Base ’n Break and other setups—and publicly references 10/20 EMA, not a standalone 5/20 crossover (official site; Kell’s commentary).

SPX read: triple-barrier expectancy −0.012R and PF 0.98. Decision: rename as generic EMA crossover; do not ship under Kell’s name.

Escalator

Name conflict
09

Implemented: 20-day prior-channel breakout with a stateful 3×ATR14 ratcheting stop; short side mirrored.

Source audit: the thread leaves N and multiplier unspecified. Worse, a catalog based on the Omega Research System Trading & Development Club describes classic Escalator as a two-bar reversal price pattern, not this channel system (TaCuda catalog). This result is best read as a generic Donchian/ATR proxy.

SPX read: +0.175R full-history and +0.229R holdout EV, but only ten independent holdout events and a wide interval. Decision: advance the generic channel/ATR concept, reject the unsupported brand label.

Checkmate

Proprietary-name proxy
10

Implemented: close beyond the prior 40-day channel while ATR20 > 1.1×ATR60; symmetric direction and persistent position state.

Source audit: public vendor material describes Checkmate only as a selective, mid-term, multi-market trend system with advanced filtering and combined exits; it does not disclose the thread’s 40/20/60 formula (Trade Center description). The actual rules appear proprietary.

SPX read: only 11 signals, −0.318R and PF 0.56. Decision: reject this proxy as presently specified; the sample is also too small.

Which is most popular?

Answer: Turtle Trading is the most popular recognizable system among these ten. It has a famous historical experiment, published mechanical rules, books, many independent implementations and a direct link to the institutional trend-following/CTA tradition. The broader moving-average crossover concept is arguably more ubiquitous as an indicator, and academic work explicitly calls the double-crossover MA rule “very popular,” but the thread’s specific “Oliver-Kell EMA5/20 system” is not a well-supported named system.

The conclusion is qualitative rather than a claim about live assets under management. Supporting evidence includes the published Turtle rules and numerous open-source implementations, AIMA’s use of the Turtle experiment as a canonical systematic CTA example, and long-horizon research on trend following. AQR’s study finds time-series momentum broadly across asset classes and decades, but that evidence supports the strategy family—not this SPX backtest or any one parameter set (AIMA; AQR). For moving averages, Baltussen, van Bekkum and Da (2021) explicitly frame the double-crossover rule as very popular while noting mostly negative academic conclusions on technical-rule profitability.

TradeAgent skill roadmap

Advance to native prototype

  • Turtle signal skill: versioned System 1, then add System 2, prior-winner filter and explicit N metadata.
  • Generic channel + ATR skill: test the Escalator-proxy behavior without the unsupported name.
  • Generic EMA crossover: useful negative baseline/control, not branded as Oliver Kell.

Prototype after more data

  • Dual Thrust: canonical-vs-article formula switch fixed at development time, then multi-year intraday data.
  • Fairy Four Price: explicit session close and ATR stop.
  • Dolphin proxy: rename EMA momentum breakout; compare 5m, 15m and 30m histories.
  • R-Breaker: implement the full six-level state machine.

Defer or reject

  • Martingale: no directional-signal skill; only a separate capped ruin simulator if explicitly requested.
  • Checkmate: do not borrow a proprietary system name for an unauthenticated formula.
  • Gap: retain as a parameterized experiment until execution-aware fill tests establish a stable edge.
